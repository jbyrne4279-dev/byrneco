Surveys for Flats & Leasehold Conversions in London

A huge proportion of London's flats are converted from Victorian and Edwardian houses. Here's what a survey covers, what it can't, and what else you need to check.

Do Flats Need a Survey?

Yes — the assumption that flats are "lower risk" than houses because they're often smaller or newer inside is a common and costly mistake. Many London flats are conversions of much older buildings, meaning the roof, external walls and structure are frequently just as old as a period house, even if your individual unit has been freshly refurbished.

Scenario

Top-floor flat, converted loft

You're responsible for a share of the roof even though it's "not your flat's structure" on paper. A survey checks roof condition and flags any signs of past leaks affecting your ceiling.

Scenario

Ground-floor flat, period conversion

Ground floors often show damp or subsidence issues first. A survey checks for movement and damp specific to your unit and shared structural elements.

What a Survey Covers in a Flat

  • Condition of your specific unit — walls, ceilings, floors, windows, fixtures
  • Visible condition of communal areas you have access to (hallways, stairwells)
  • Externally visible condition of the whole building's roof, walls and windows
  • Signs of damp, movement or disrepair that could indicate wider building issues
  • Evidence of poor past conversion work — inadequate soundproofing, fire separation, or structural alterations

What a Survey Can't Tell You

A Building Survey is about physical condition, not legal or financial standing. It won't cover:

  • The length remaining on the lease, or the cost of extending it
  • Service charge history, reserve fund adequacy, or major works planned by the freeholder
  • Ground rent terms and any onerous lease clauses
  • Building insurance arrangements held by the freeholder or management company
  • Whether the freeholder/management company is well run and solvent
Always request these separately Ask your solicitor to obtain the management pack, service charge accounts, and a copy of the lease early in the process — ideally before your survey, so any red flags can be discussed alongside the physical condition findings.

Questions to Ask Your Managing Agent or Freeholder

  • Is any major works programme planned or underway (roof, lift, external redecoration)?
  • What is the current service charge, and has it increased significantly in recent years?
  • Who is responsible for the roof, external walls and structure under the lease?
  • Is there a sinking/reserve fund, and how much is currently held?
  • Has the building had any insurance claims for subsidence, fire or water damage?

Which Survey Level for a Flat?

A Level 2 HomeBuyer Report is often sufficient for a flat in a well-maintained purpose-built or professionally managed block in reasonable condition. A Level 3 Building Survey is the safer choice for period conversions, older buildings, or flats where you have concerns about the roof, structure or any visible disrepair in communal areas.
