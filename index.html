Survey Levels

Which Survey Level Do You Need?

Not sure whether you need a Level 2 or Level 3 survey? Read our full guide to choosing between them, or ask us directly — free of charge.

Level 1 — Condition Report

A visual-only inspection with traffic-light condition ratings, flagging urgent defects and legal risks without advice on repairs or costs. Suits conventional, newer properties already in good condition where you want a quick, independent check before exchanging.

Get Quote

Level 2 — HomeBuyer Report

Adds defect and maintenance advice plus an extended roof space and drainage inspection over Level 1, with an optional market valuation and insurance reinstatement figure. Suits standard homes under around 50 years old, built from common materials, simple in layout and in reasonable condition.

Get Quote

Level 3 — Building Survey

Establishes how the property is built and how it will perform, describing both visible and hidden defect risks with repair options, timelines and the cost of inaction. Recommended for large, older, altered or run-down properties, unusual construction, or anywhere you plan major works — the majority of London's housing stock.

FAQs

Common Questions About Buying with a Survey

How long does a house survey take?

The on-site inspection typically takes 2–4 hours, depending on the size and complexity of the property. Your written report is usually delivered within 5 working days of the inspection.

When should you get a survey on a property?

As soon as your offer has been accepted. Getting a survey booked early gives you time to use any findings to renegotiate the price, request repairs, or reconsider the purchase before contracts are exchanged.

Who organises a survey when buying a house?

You do, as the buyer. You're welcome to use a surveyor recommended by your estate agent or lender, but you don't have to — you can instruct any RICS-regulated firm and it's worth comparing quotes.

How long does it take from survey to completion?

There's no fixed timeline — it depends on the report's findings, any renegotiation, and the wider conveyancing process, which typically takes 8–12 weeks in total from offer to completion.

Is a survey required to sell a house?

Not in England and Wales, though some sellers commission one to identify issues before marketing. In Scotland, sellers must provide a Home Report — including a Level 2 survey and valuation — before the property can be marketed.
